🛤️ Parallel Lines and a Transversal

Discover the secret patterns created when one line slices through two others!

The 8 Angles of a Transversal

A Transversal is simply a line that passes through two or more other lines. When it cuts across two perfectly Parallel Lines, it creates 8 distinct angles that share incredibly special and predictable relationships.

Angle Pair Type Relationship (If lines are parallel)
Corresponding Angles Angles in the exact same position at each intersection. They are always Equal.
Alternate Interior Angles Angles on opposite sides of the transversal and between the two lines. They are always Equal.
Alternate Exterior Angles Angles on opposite sides of the transversal and outside the two lines. They are always Equal.
Co-Interior Angles Angles on the same side of the transversal and between the two lines. They always Sum to 180 degrees.

✅ 2. The Parallel Line Test

We know that if lines are parallel, Alternate Interior Angles are equal. But this works in reverse too! Tilt the bottom line. The very moment the two interior angles match, the lines become perfectly parallel.

Angles do not match. Lines will intersect!

🤝 3. The Co-Interior Sum

Angles on the same side of the transversal (between the parallel lines) are called Co-Interior or Consecutive Interior angles. Move the transversal and watch how they always work together to reach exactly 180 degrees.

Co-Interior Angle Sum 110° + 70° = 180°

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: If two lines are parallel and a transversal creates a corresponding angle of 55 degrees, what is the measurement of the alternate interior angle?
Step 1: Corresponding angles are equal. So the angle directly "sliding down" the transversal is also 55 degrees.
Step 2: Alternate interior angles are vertically opposite to corresponding angles, making them equal as well.
Final Answer: 55 degrees. In parallel lines, all acute angles created by the transversal are identical!
Q2: If interior angles on the same side of a transversal sum to 178 degrees, what does that tell us about the two lines?
They are NOT parallel!

Because 178 degrees is less than 180 degrees, Euclid's 5th Postulate guarantees that if you extend those two lines, they will eventually crash into each other on that exact side of the transversal.
