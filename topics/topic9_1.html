The Parts of a Circle

A circle is a collection of all points in a plane which are at a constant distance (radius) from a fixed point (center). Let's learn the vocabulary required to describe its different sections.

Term Definition
Radius A line segment connecting the center to any point on the circle.
Chord A line segment connecting any two distinct points on the circle.
Diameter The longest possible chord. It passes directly through the center and is exactly twice the length of the radius.
Arc A continuous piece of the circle's outer edge (circumference).
Sector & Segment A Sector is a slice bounded by two radii (like a pizza slice). A Segment is a region bounded by a chord and an arc.

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

If you connect the center to the two endpoints of any chord, you form a triangle with two sides equal to the radius (r). The Triangle Inequality Theorem dictates that the length of the third side (the chord) must be strictly less than or equal to the sum of the other two sides (r + r = 2r). The only way the chord reaches its absolute maximum length of 2r is when the "triangle" perfectly flattens out, meaning the chord passes straight through the center. A chord passing through the center is exactly what we call a diameter!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: If the radius of a circle is 8.5 cm, what is the length of its longest chord?
17 cm.

The longest chord of a circle is its diameter, which is exactly twice the length of the radius (8.5 × 2 = 17).
Q2: What is the difference between a sector and a segment?
A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc (like a slice of pizza). A segment is bounded by a single chord and an arc.
