📐 Area of a Triangle

Calculate 2D space using base and perpendicular height!

The Master Formula

The area of a triangle measures the total 2D space enclosed within its three sides. No matter how wildly a triangle is slanted or skewed, calculating its area relies on only two fundamental measurements: the Base and the strict Perpendicular Height.

Component Definition
Base (b) Any one of the three sides of the triangle chosen as the bottom anchor.
Perpendicular Height (h) The straight 90° line dropped from the opposite vertex directly down to the base (or extended base).
Area Formula Area = ½ × Base × Height

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

By dropping a perpendicular height, we slice the main triangle into two smaller right-angled triangles. If we perfectly duplicate and rotate these two smaller triangles around their hypotenuses, they fill in the empty space above to create a perfect bounding rectangle! The area of a rectangle is simply Base × Height. Since our original triangle makes up exactly half of the blocks forming this rectangle, its area must strictly be ½ × Base × Height!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: A triangle has a base of 15 cm and a perpendicular height of 10 cm. What is its total area?
75 cm².

Using the formula: Area = ½ × base × height.
Area = ½ × 15 × 10 = 75 cm².
Q2: If you pull the top vertex of a triangle far to the right, heavily slanting its sides but keeping the base and perpendicular height exactly the same, does the area increase?
No, the area remains exactly the same!

As demonstrated in the first simulator, the area formula relies strictly on the horizontal base and the vertical perpendicular height. The steepness or slant of the bounding perimeter does not alter the total amount of 2D space enclosed inside!
