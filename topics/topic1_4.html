🌪️ Irrational Numbers

The rebel numbers that refuse to be simple fractions!

What makes a number Irrational?

While rational numbers are clean, orderly, and can always be written as a fraction (p/q), irrational numbers are exactly the opposite. They represent quantities that cannot be perfectly captured by simple division.

Characteristic The Rule
Fraction Rule CANNOT be written in the form p/q (where p and q are integers).
Decimal Expansion They have Non-Terminating (never-ending) AND Non-Repeating (no regular pattern) decimals.
Famous Examples π (Pi): The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter.
√2, √3, √5: Square roots of non-perfect squares.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: We often use 22/7 as the value for Pi (π). Doesn't that make π a rational number?
No! π is strictly irrational.

22/7 is just an approximation we use to make calculations easier. If you divide 22 by 7, you get a repeating decimal (3.142857...). The actual value of π (3.14159...) never repeats and never ends.
Q2: If you multiply two irrational numbers together, will the answer always be irrational?
No, it can be rational!

Step 1: Take the irrational number √2.
Step 2: Multiply it by itself: √2 × √2.
Step 3: The roots cancel out, leaving exactly 2.
Final Answer: Since 2 is a rational number, multiplying two irrationals can sometimes yield a perfectly rational result!
