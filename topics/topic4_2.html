📈 Graph of a Linear Equation

Translating algebraic equations into geometric lines!

From Algebra to Geometry

A linear equation in two variables forms a perfectly straight line on the Cartesian plane. There is a direct, unbreakable link between the algebra (the numbers) and the geometry (the picture).

Algebraic Concept Geometric Meaning
A Solution (x, y) A specific point that lies exactly on the line.
Infinite Solutions A line extends infinitely in both directions, made up of an infinite number of points.
Plotting the Graph You only need to find two solutions (two points) to draw the entire line! (Finding a third point is a great way to check your work).

🎯 1. Graphing via Intercepts

The easiest way to graph a line is to find where it crosses the X-axis (by setting y = 0) and the Y-axis (by setting x = 0). Adjust the equation below to see how the intercepts form the line!

🎢 2. The Sliding Solution

Because a line is continuous, every single point lying on it represents a valid decimal or fractional solution to the equation. Slide the point along the line x + y = 5 to verify this!

Checking the Solution: x + y = 5 ✅ It Balances!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: If a point (3, 4) lies on the graph of the equation 3y = ax + 7, find the value of a.
Step 1: Since the point lies on the line, it must satisfy the equation. Substitute x = 3 and y = 4 into the equation.
Step 2: 3(4) = a(3) + 7
Step 3: 12 = 3a + 7
Step 4: 12 - 7 = 3a ➡️ 5 = 3a ➡️ a = 5/3.
Final Answer: a = 5/3
Q2: The graph of the linear equation x = 4 is a line parallel to which axis?
It is parallel to the Y-axis.

The equation x = 4 means that no matter what the y-value is, the x-value is always securely locked at 4. This creates a vertical line that never crosses the Y-axis (which is the line x = 0).
