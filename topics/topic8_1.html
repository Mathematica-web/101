⏹️ Introduction to Quadrilaterals

Meet the family of four-sided polygons!

What is a Quadrilateral?

A polygon formed by joining exactly four distinct points (where no three are collinear) is called a Quadrilateral (from Latin: quadri meaning four, and latus meaning side). While any random 4-sided shape is a quadrilateral, mathematicians have categorized them into a special family based on parallel lines, equal sides, and equal angles.

Shape Name Defining Feature
Trapezium Exactly one pair of opposite sides are parallel.
Parallelogram Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
Rectangle A parallelogram where all four angles are exactly 90°.
Rhombus A parallelogram where all four sides are exactly equal in length.
Square The perfect shape: All four sides are equal AND all four angles are 90°.
Kite Two distinct pairs of adjacent (next to each other) sides are equal.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Is a Square considered a type of Rectangle?
Yes!

A rectangle is defined strictly as a parallelogram where all four angles are 90°. A square perfectly meets this requirement! A square is simply a specialized rectangle that *also* happens to have all four sides equal. (It is also a specialized rhombus!).
Q2: If the diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other at exactly 90°, what shape could it be?
It must be a Rhombus or a Square.

While a Kite's diagonals cross at 90°, only *one* of its diagonals is bisected. Only Rhombuses and Squares feature diagonals that both bisect each other AND intersect at 90° right angles.
