📉 Measures of Central Tendency

Finding the "center" of your data with Mean, Median, and Mode!

The "Big Three" Averages

When you have a massive list of numbers, finding a single value that represents the entire dataset is crucial. Depending on your data, one of these three measures will be the most useful:

Measure Definition & Formula (Ungrouped) When to Use
Mean (Average) Sum of all observations / Total number of observations.

x̄ = Σxi / n		 When data is evenly spread out with no extreme "outliers".
Median (Middle) The middle value when data is sorted in ascending order.

If n is even, average the two middle terms.		 When data is skewed or contains extreme high/low values.
Mode (Most Frequent) The observation that occurs most often. When determining the most popular choice (e.g., most sold shoe size).

⚖️ 2. The Empirical Relationship

In moderately skewed distributions, the Mean, Median, and Mode are mathematically linked. If you know two of them, you can find the third using the Empirical Formula:
3 × Median = Mode + 2 × Mean

Estimated Mode = (3 × Median) - (2 × Mean) 30

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: In a class of 5 students, four students score 80% on a test, and one student scores 0% because they were absent. Which measure of central tendency gives the fairest picture of the class's performance?
The Median (or Mode)!

The scores are: 0, 80, 80, 80, 80.
The Mean is (320 / 5) = 64%, which doesn't reflect the majority.
The Median is 80%, and the Mode is 80%, both of which show that the typical student performed very well, completely ignoring the single outlier (0%).
Q2: If the Mode of a dataset is 20 and the Mean is 35, use the Empirical Formula to find the Median.
Step 1: Write the formula: 3(Median) = Mode + 2(Mean)
Step 2: Substitute values: 3(Median) = 20 + 2(35)
Step 3: Simplify: 3(Median) = 20 + 70 = 90
Step 4: Solve: Median = 90 / 3
Final Answer: Median = 30
