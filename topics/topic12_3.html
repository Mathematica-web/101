Measuring the likelihood of events, from coin tosses to drawing cards!

The Core Probability Rules

Probability is simply a fraction that describes how likely something is to happen. Theoretical Probability is what we expect to happen logically. Experimental Probability is what actually happens when we run an experiment.

Concept Rule / Formula
Probability Formula P(E) = Number of favorable outcomes / Total possible outcomes
The Limits Probability is always between 0 and 1. 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
Certain & Impossible A Sure Event has a probability of exactly 1 (100%).
An Impossible Event has a probability of exactly 0.
Complementary Events The probability of an event happening plus it NOT happening is always 1.
P(E) + P(Not E) = 1

🪙 1. The Coin Flipper (Law of Large Numbers)

Theoretical probability says a fair coin will land on Heads 1/2 of the time. But if you flip it just 3 times, you might get 3/3 Heads! The Law of Large Numbers states that as you run more experiments (flip 100s of times), the experimental probability fraction will get closer and closer to the theoretical 1/2.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: What is the probability of rolling a 7 on a standard six-sided die?
0 (An Impossible Event)

A standard die only has the numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6. Because there are no 7s, the number of favorable outcomes is 0.
0 / 6 = 0.
Q2: The weather forecast states there is a 65% chance of rain today. What is the probability that it will NOT rain?
Step 1: Convert 65% to a decimal probability: 0.65.
Step 2: Use the Complementary Event rule: P(E) + P(Not E) = 1.
Step 3: 0.65 + P(Not Rain) = 1.
Step 4: P(Not Rain) = 1 - 0.65 = 0.35.
Final Answer: 0.35 (or a 35% chance).
