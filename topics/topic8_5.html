🛠️ Converse of the Mid-point Theorem

Reversing the logic to solve complex geometry!

Theorem 8.10 (Converse)

The line drawn through the mid-point of one side of a triangle, parallel to another side, bisects the third side.

If D is the mid-point of AB, and we draw DE || BC, then E will automatically be the mid-point of AC.

🌟 Application: Quadrilaterals inside Quadrilaterals!

If you join the mid-points of adjacent sides of ANY quadrilateral, the figure formed inside is ALWAYS a parallelogram! This beautiful rule is known as Varignon's Theorem.

📝 Mathematical Proof

Given: Quadrilateral ABCD. P, Q, R, and S are mid-points of sides AB, BC, CD, and DA respectively.

To prove: PQRS is a parallelogram.

StatementReason
Draw the diagonal AC.Construction
In ΔABC, P and Q are mid-points of sides AB and BC.Given
PQ || AC and PQ = ½ ACMid-point Theorem applied to ΔABC
In ΔADC, S and R are mid-points of sides AD and CD.Given
SR || AC and SR = ½ ACMid-point Theorem applied to ΔADC
PQ || SRSince both are parallel to AC (Lines parallel to same line are parallel to each other)
PQ = SRSince both are equal to ½ AC (Things equal to same thing are equal)
In quadrilateral PQRS, one pair of opposite sides (PQ and SR) is equal and parallel.Proven in the steps above
∴ PQRS is a parallelogram. ✅Condition for a parallelogram (Theorem 8.8)
