📉 Tangents and their Lengths

Touching the circle at exactly one microscopic point!

The Rules of the Tangent

A tangent is a perfectly straight line that touches the outside of a circle at exactly one single point (the point of contact). It never cuts inside. Because it perfectly balances on the edge, it creates two major theorems.

Theorem The Rule
Theorem 10.1 (Perpendicularity) The tangent at any point of a circle is strictly perpendicular (90°) to the radius drawn through the point of contact.
Theorem 10.2 (External Lengths) The lengths of two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are exactly equal.

📐 1. The Tangent-Radius Lock

Drag the point of contact around the circle. Notice how the tangent line perfectly rotates to remain mathematically locked at exactly 90° to the radius!

Radius OP ⊥ Tangent Line

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point (P). If you pick any other point (let's say Q) on this tangent line, Q must lie outside the circle. This means the distance from the Center (O) to Q is always greater than the radius OP. Because OP is the absolute shortest possible distance from the center to the tangent line, it geometrically must be strictly perpendicular (90°) to the line!

✂️ 2. Equal External Tangents

Move the external Point P. The system mathematically calculates the exact two points where lines from P gracefully brush the circle. Notice how the lengths of PA and PB are always perfectly identical!

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

Imagine drawing a line from the external point P to the Center. This creates two right-angled triangles: ΔPAO and ΔPBO. Because of Theorem 10.1, we know the angles at A and B are exactly 90°. Both triangles share the exact same hypotenuse (the line PO). Also, both triangles have one side that is the radius of the circle (OA = OB). By the RHS (Right-Angle Hypotenuse Side) congruence rule, the two triangles are mathematically identical! Therefore, their remaining sides (the tangents PA and PB) MUST be completely equal in length.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: How many tangents can be drawn to a circle from a single point located exactly on the circle's edge?
Exactly one.

A tangent can only touch the circle at one specific point of contact. Any additional line drawn through that same point would slice inside the circle, becoming a secant.
Q2: Two tangents are drawn to a circle from an external point P. If the first tangent is 15 cm long, how long is the second one?
15 cm.

Theorem 10.2 explicitly proves that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are always perfectly equal.
