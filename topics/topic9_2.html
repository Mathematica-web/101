📏 Properties of Chords

Discovering symmetry and angles hidden inside the circle!

The Chord Theorems

Because a circle is perfectly symmetrical around its center, the straight lines drawn inside it (chords) obey incredibly strict, predictable rules.

Theorem The Rule
Equal Chords & Equal Angles Equal chords of a circle subtend (create) perfectly equal angles at the center. The converse is also true: if central angles are equal, the chords must be equal.
The Perpendicular Bisector The perpendicular drawn from the center of a circle to a chord perfectly bisects it (cuts it into two equal halves).

📐 1. Equal Chords Subtend Equal Angles

Adjust the lengths of the two chords. The moment you make Chord 1 exactly equal to Chord 2, watch how their central angles mathematically lock to the exact same value!

Chords are unequal. Central angles differ.

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

Drawing lines from the center to the ends of the chords forms two triangles. The sides of these triangles are radii of the circle, meaning two pairs of sides are already equal. When you adjust the sliders to make the chords equal, the third pair of sides match. By the SSS (Side-Side-Side) Congruence Rule, the two triangles become perfectly identical! Therefore, their central angles must be exactly equal by CPCT.

📉 2. The Perpendicular Bisector

Move the chord up and down. A straight perpendicular line dropped from the center will always slice the chord exactly in half, creating two perfectly equal segments!

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

Imagine drawing two radii connecting the center to the ends of the chord. This forms two small right-angled triangles. Both triangles share the perpendicular line as a common height. Their hypotenuses are both radii of the circle, meaning they are equal. By the RHS (Right-Angle Hypotenuse Side) congruence rule, the two triangles are mathematically identical, meaning their base segments must be exactly equal!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Two chords in a circle, AB and CD, both measure 10 cm. If chord AB subtends an angle of 50° at the center, what angle does CD subtend at the center?
50°.

According to the chord theorems, equal chords of a circle always subtend perfectly equal angles at the center.
Q2: A perpendicular is dropped from the center of a circle to a chord of length 24 cm. What are the lengths of the two segments it creates?
12 cm each.

The perpendicular from the center to a chord perfectly bisects it, splitting the 24 cm chord into two equal 12 cm halves.
