🧩 Introduction to Polynomials

Building algebraic chains using constants and variables!

The Vocabulary of Algebra

A polynomial is an algebraic expression made up of terms that are added or subtracted together. To master polynomials, you first need to learn the language!

Term Definition
Variable A letter (usually x, y, or z) that represents an unknown or changing value.
Coefficient The regular number multiplied directly in front of a variable. (If there is no number, the coefficient is 1!)
Constant A fixed number standing alone with no variables attached (e.g., the + 7 in 2x + 7).
Degree The highest exponent of the variable in the entire polynomial. This single number decides the polynomial's name and behavior.
Strict Rule For an expression to be a true polynomial, the exponents of the variables must be Whole Numbers. (No negative exponents, no fractions, no square roots of variables!)

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Which of the following expressions is NOT a polynomial?
A) 3x² + 2x
B) 5x³ - 4/x
C) 7x - 9
Expression B is NOT a polynomial!

Look closely at the term -4/x.
When a variable is in the denominator, it represents a negative exponent (in this case, -4x⁻¹).
Polynomial rules strictly state that all exponents must be whole numbers (0, 1, 2, 3...). Negative exponents are not allowed!
Q2: What is the degree of the polynomial: p(y) = 4 - 5y² + y⁴?
Step 1: Identify all the terms and their exponents.
The terms are 4 (exponent 0), -5y² (exponent 2), and y⁴ (exponent 4).
Step 2: Find the highest exponent present.
The highest exponent is 4.
Final Answer: The degree is 4 (Quartic Polynomial).
