🎯 Zeroes of a Polynomial

Finding the magic numbers that make the polynomial vanish!

What is a "Zero"?

A zero (or root) of a polynomial p(x) is simply any number c that, when plugged in for x, makes the entire equation equal to exactly 0. Visually, this is where the graph crashes into the X-axis!

Concept Explanation
Mathematical Definition If p(c) = 0, then c is a zero of the polynomial p(x).
Geometrical Meaning The x-intercepts. Every time the line or curve crosses the horizontal X-axis, the y-value is 0. That x-coordinate is your zero!
The Degree Rule A polynomial of degree n can have at most n real zeroes. (A linear equation has 1, a quadratic can have up to 2, a cubic up to 3).

📏 1. The Linear Root Finder

A Linear Polynomial (Degree 1) forms a perfectly straight line and crosses the X-axis exactly once. Adjust the slope (a) and constant (b) to see how the zero changes!

p(x) = 1x + 0
The Zero (x-intercept) x = 0 Calculated as x = -b / a

🧲 2. The Parabola Bender

A Quadratic Polynomial (Degree 2) forms a U-shaped curve called a Parabola. Depending on where it sits, it can crash through the X-axis twice, touch it once, or miss it completely! Adjust the Constant (c) to shift it up and down.

p(x) = x² + 0x - 4
Number of Real Zeroes 2 x = -2, x = 2

🔗 3. The Root-Coefficient Link

Even if you don't know the exact zeroes, you can easily calculate their Sum and Product just by looking at the coefficients! This magic trick is known as Vieta's Formulas.

Sum of Zeroes (-b / a) -4 (α + β)
Sum of Prod (c / a) -5 (αβ + βγ + γα)
Product of Zeroes (c / a) -5 (α × β)

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Find the zero of the linear polynomial p(x) = 2x - 6.
Step 1: To find a zero, set the polynomial equal to 0.
2x - 6 = 0
Step 2: Add 6 to both sides.
2x = 6
Step 3: Divide by 2.
x = 6 / 2 = 3.
Final Answer: The zero is 3.
Q2: Without finding the actual roots, what is the sum and product of the zeroes for the quadratic p(x) = 3x² - 12x + 9?
Step 1: Identify coefficients: a = 3, b = -12, c = 9.
Step 2: Calculate the Sum using (-b / a).
-(-12) / 3 = 12 / 3 = 4.
Step 3: Calculate the Product using (c / a).
9 / 3 = 3.
Final Answer: The sum of the roots is 4, and the product is 3.
