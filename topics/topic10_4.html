🟦 Area of Parallelograms

The master formula for finding the area of a slanted rectangle!

The Core Formula

A parallelogram looks like a rectangle that has been pushed over. But does this slanted shape hold less space than a straight rectangle? The answer lies in its perpendicular height.

Component Definition
Base (b) Any of the flat, parallel sides of the parallelogram.
Perpendicular Height (h) The straight 90° distance between the base and its opposite parallel side. (Never use the slanted side!)
Area Formula Area = Base * Height

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

The most common mistake is multiplying the base by the slanted side! The slanted side is always longer than the perpendicular height (because it forms the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle). By locking the perpendicular height and the base, the shape occupies exactly the same space as a rectangle of identical dimensions. The slant just shifts the area horizontally!

Area = Base * Height

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

If you drop a perpendicular line from the top-left corner of a parallelogram, it creates a small right-angled triangle. Because opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal and parallel, this sliced triangle fits perfectly into the empty space on the opposite side. When you move it, the shape magically transforms into a perfect rectangle. The formula for a rectangle's area is Base * Height. Since no area was added or lost during the morph, the area of the parallelogram must also be identically Base * Height!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: A parallelogram has a base of 15 cm, a slanted side of 10 cm, and a perpendicular height of 8 cm. What is its total area?
120 sq cm.

The area is Base * Height (15 * 8 = 120). The slanted side (10 cm) is extra information designed to distract you. You never use the slanted side to calculate area!
Q2: How does the area of a parallelogram compare to a rectangle if both share the exact same base and the exact same height?
They are exactly the same!

As demonstrated in the Rectangle Morph simulator, a parallelogram can be physically cut and rearranged into a rectangle of the identical base and height, proving their areas are equal.
