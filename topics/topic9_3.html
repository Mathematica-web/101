📏 Angle Subtended by an Arc

Exploring the elegant mathematical relationships between the center and the edge!

Center vs. Circumference

If you take any arc (a curved piece of the circle) and use it to draw an angle to the exact center, and then draw another angle to anywhere on the remaining outer edge, fundamental rules of geometry emerge.

Theorem The Rule
The Double Angle Theorem The angle subtended by an arc at the center is exactly double the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle.
Angles in the Same Segment Angles subtended by the same arc at any two points in the same segment of the circle are perfectly equal.
Angle in a Semicircle The angle subtended by a diameter at any point on the circumference is always exactly 90° (a right angle).

📐 1. The Double Angle Theorem

The bottom arc AB subtends a fixed 120° angle at the Center. Drag point C around the outer edge. Notice how, no matter where C moves, the angle at the circumference remains mathematically locked at exactly half (60°)!

Angle at Center ∠AOB = 120°
Angle at Circumference ∠ACB = 60°

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

If you were to draw a line from the Center to point C, you would split the large shape into two triangles. Because the lines from the center to A, B, and C are all radii, both triangles are isosceles! By applying the Exterior Angle Theorem to these isosceles triangles, the mathematics algebraically simplifies perfectly to prove that the central angle must always be exactly double the angle at the edge.

🔗 2. Angles in the Same Segment

Because the angle at the edge is always exactly half the angle at the center, it stands to reason that any two angles drawn from the same arc will equal each other. Slide Points C and D independently to prove they always match!

∠ACB = ∠ADB = 60°

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

Imagine drawing invisible lines from the center of the circle to points A and B. This creates a central angle AOB. According to the Double Angle Theorem (from the first simulator), the angle at the center is always exactly twice the angle at the edge. This mathematically dictates that ∠AOB = 2 × ∠ACB, AND ∠AOB = 2 × ∠ADB. Since both angles at the edge are exactly half of the same central angle, they must be perfectly equal to each other!

⭕ 3. Angle in a Semicircle

What happens when the arc is exactly half the circle? The "central angle" is a flat line (180°). Therefore, by the Double Angle Theorem, the angle at the edge must be exactly half of 180°. It is always 90°!

The angle in a semicircle is always a right angle!

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

This is a brilliant shortcut derived directly from the Double Angle Theorem! Because points A and B are connected by a diameter, the line passing through the center is perfectly straight. A straight line forms an angle of exactly 180°. Since the angle at the circumference (Angle C) must be exactly half of the central angle, it mathematically calculates to 180° ÷ 2 = 90°.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: An arc subtends an angle of 130° at the center of the circle. What angle does it subtend at a point on the remaining circumference?
65°.

The Double Angle Theorem states the angle at the edge is exactly half the angle at the center (130° ÷ 2 = 65°).
Q2: Why is the angle in a semicircle always 90°?
A semicircle's arc subtends a straight line (180°) at the center. Since the angle at the circumference is half the central angle, 180° ÷ 2 = 90°.
