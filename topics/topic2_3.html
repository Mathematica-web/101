🪄 Remainder & Factor Theorems

The ultimate algebraic shortcuts to bypass long division!

The Magic Shortcuts

Dividing polynomials using traditional long division takes forever. Mathematicians discovered two incredible rules that let you find the remainder—or check if a polynomial perfectly divides another—just by plugging in a single number!

Theorem The Rule
Remainder Theorem If a polynomial p(x) is divided by a linear polynomial (x - a), the remainder is simply p(a). Just plug the opposite sign into the equation!
Factor Theorem A special case of the Remainder Theorem! If p(a) = 0, then (x - a) is a perfect factor of the polynomial (meaning no remainder).
The Catch This shortcut ONLY works when dividing by a linear term (like x - 3 or 2x + 1). You cannot use it to divide by x²!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: What is the remainder when p(x) = x³ + 1 is divided by (x + 1)?
Step 1: Identify the divisor, which is (x + 1). Set it to 0 to find the root: x = -1.
Step 2: Plug -1 into the polynomial p(x).
p(-1) = (-1)³ + 1
Step 3: Calculate.
p(-1) = -1 + 1 = 0.
Final Answer: The remainder is 0. This means (x + 1) is a perfect factor of x³ + 1.
Q2: Can I use the Remainder Theorem to divide a polynomial by (x² - 2)?
No, you cannot.

The Remainder Theorem strictly requires the divisor to be a linear polynomial (degree 1), meaning it must be in the form (x - a) or (ax + b). Because (x² - 2) has a degree of 2, the magic shortcut fails, and you must use standard long division!
