📏 Same Base & Parallels

How shapes can stretch and warp without changing their total area!

The Parallel Corridor

Imagine two perfectly parallel lines acting like a rigid corridor. If you anchor the base of a shape on one line, and touch the top of the shape to the other line, you lock in its height. Because of this, we discover three powerful rules about shapes sharing the same space.

Theorem The Rule
Theorem 1 (Parallelograms) Parallelograms on the same base and between the same parallels are exactly equal in area.
Theorem 2 (Triangles) Two triangles on the same base and between the same parallels are exactly equal in area.
Theorem 3 (Mixed Shapes) If a triangle and a parallelogram share the same base and parallels, the triangle's area is exactly half of the parallelogram's area.

🟧 1. The Parallelogram Stretcher

Drag the top edge of the parallelogram left and right. Although the shape skews wildly and its side lengths change, the total 2D space inside remains mathematically locked!

Area of Parallelogram Base × Height = 30000 sq units

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

The area of any parallelogram is calculated strictly as Base × Perpendicular Height. Because the shape is trapped between two parallel lines, its perpendicular height can never change. Because we anchored the base, its width can never change. Therefore, regardless of how far you skew the top, the Area remains identically Base × Height.

🔺 2. The Triangle Slider

The same rule applies to triangles! Slide the top vertex of the triangle anywhere along the top parallel line. The area will always be exactly half of the parallelogram from above.

Area of Triangle 1/2 × Base × Height = 15000 sq units

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

A triangle's area formula is 1/2 * Base * Height. Just like the parallelogram, the distance between the two parallel lines represents the constant height of the triangle. Since both the base and the height are physically locked, moving the top point changes the triangle's shape (its perimeter and angles) but its area cannot mathematically change!

⚖️ 3. The 1:2 Ratio Showdown

When a triangle and a parallelogram share the exact same base and the exact same height, the triangle will ALWAYS consume exactly 50% of the parallelogram's area. Move the triangle's vertex to see this in action!

Parallelogram Area 30000 units
Triangle Area 15000 units

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

This is a direct comparison of their foundational formulas! The Parallelogram's area is (Base * Height). The Triangle's area is (1/2 * Base * Height). Because both shapes are placed on the exact same base and trapped between the exact same parallel lines, their "Base" and "Height" variables are identical. Therefore, the triangle's formula dictates it must be exactly half the size of the parallelogram!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Two different parallelograms are drawn on a base of 10 cm between parallel lines that are 8 cm apart. What is the difference in their areas?
Zero!

Because they share the same base (10 cm) and the same height (8 cm), Theorem 1 guarantees their areas are perfectly identical (80 sq cm each). The difference is 0.
Q2: A triangle and a rectangle sit on the same base of 5 units and fit between the same parallel lines. If the rectangle has an area of 40 sq units, what is the triangle's area?
20 sq units.

A rectangle is just a special type of parallelogram. According to Theorem 3, any triangle on the same base and between the same parallels as a parallelogram will have exactly half the area. 40 / 2 = 20.
