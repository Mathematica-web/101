➗ Division of Polynomials

Slicing big equations into smaller, organized pieces!

The Division Algorithm

Just like regular numbers, when you divide one polynomial by another, you get a quotient and sometimes a remainder. The universal rule connecting them all is called the Division Algorithm.

Component Role in the Algorithm
Dividend (p(x)) The massive polynomial being sliced up inside the division bracket.
Divisor (g(x)) The polynomial doing the slicing on the outside.
The Golden Equation Dividend = (Divisor × Quotient) + Remainder

p(x) = g(x) · q(x) + r(x)
The Stopping Rule You must stop dividing the moment the Degree of the Remainder becomes less than the Degree of the Divisor.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Can a polynomial be divided by 0?
No!

Just like with regular numbers, division by zero is undefined in algebra. The divisor polynomial g(x) can never be equal to 0.
Q2: When dividing p(x) by a linear polynomial like (x - 3), what is the maximum possible degree of the remainder?
Step 1: Remember the stopping rule. The degree of the remainder must always be strictly less than the degree of the divisor.
Step 2: The divisor (x - 3) is a linear polynomial, which means its degree is 1.
Step 3: Therefore, the degree of the remainder must be less than 1. The only whole number less than 1 is 0.
Final Answer: The maximum degree is 0. (Meaning the remainder will always just be a constant number!)
