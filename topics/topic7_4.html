📏 Inequalities in a Triangle

Not all sides and angles are created equal!

Unequal Triangles

So far, we have looked at congruent (perfectly equal) triangles and symmetric shapes like isosceles triangles. But what happens in an ordinary, unequal triangle? Nature forces a strict set of inequalities to maintain the triangle's shape.

Inequality Theorem The Rule
Longer Side = Larger Angle In any triangle, the angle strictly opposite to the longest side is always the largest angle. Conversely, the side opposite the largest angle is always the longest side.
Triangle Inequality Theorem The sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be strictly greater than the length of the third side.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Is it possible to construct a triangle with sides measuring 3 units, 4 units, and 8 units?
No!

According to the Triangle Inequality Theorem, the sum of any two sides must be strictly greater than the third side. Here, if we add the two shorter sides (3 + 4 = 7), the sum is less than the longest side (8). The sides will never meet!
Q2: In triangle PQR, if Angle P is 80°, Angle Q is 60°, and Angle R is 40°, which side is the longest?
Side QR.

The longest side is always strictly opposite the largest angle. Since Angle P (80°) is the largest angle, the side facing it (which connects vertices Q and R) must be the longest side in the triangle.
