🔺 Properties and More Congruence Rules

Exploring Isosceles Triangles and the SSS & RHS Criteria!

Expanding the Toolkit

Beyond SAS and ASA, geometry provides powerful rules specifically for right-angled triangles and triangles constructed from three known sides. We also unlock the secret symmetry behind triangles with equal sides.

Theorem / Rule What it Means
Isosceles Triangle Theorem Angles opposite to equal sides of an isosceles triangle are equal. Conversely, sides opposite to equal angles are equal.
SSS (Side-Side-Side) If three sides of one triangle are perfectly equal to the three sides of another triangle, they are congruent.
RHS (Right-Hypotenuse-Side) If the hypotenuse and one side of a right-angled triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one side of another right-angled triangle, they are congruent.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Can we use the RHS congruence rule for any triangle?
No!

RHS specifically stands for Right Angle - Hypotenuse - Side. It only works for right-angled triangles because the presence of the 90° angle is mathematically necessary to lock the hypotenuse and leg together.
Q2: If an isosceles triangle has a vertex angle of 40°, what are the measurements of its two base angles?
70° each.

The total sum of angles in a triangle is 180°. Subtracting the top vertex angle leaves 140° (180° - 40°). According to the Isosceles Triangle Theorem, the base angles opposite the equal sides must be identical. Dividing 140° by 2 gives 70° for each base angle!
