🧩 Applying Euclid: Theorems and Proofs

Using basic rules to prove complex mathematical truths!

From Axioms to Theorems

We know that Axioms and Postulates are assumptions we accept without proof. But a Theorem is a statement that requires a logical proof. We build these proofs by stacking Axioms and Postulates together like logical lego blocks.

Proof Technique How it Works
Direct Proof Starting with known facts (Axioms) and taking logical step-by-step actions until you reach the conclusion.
Proof by Contradiction Assuming the opposite of what you want to prove is true, then showing that this assumption breaks a fundamental Axiom. If the opposite is impossible, your theorem must be true!

⚙️ 2. The Proof Logic Engine

Now let's write out the mathematical proof for Theorem 5.1 step-by-step. Click the button to advance the logical argument.

Step 1: The Setup
Let there be two distinct straight lines, l and m.
Step 2: The Contradiction Assumption
Assume the opposite of our theorem: Suppose lines l and m intersect at two distinct points, P and Q.
Step 3: Finding the Flaw
If l and m both pass through P and Q, then we have two distinct straight lines passing through the exact same two points.
Step 4: The Axiom Clash!
But Euclid's Axiom states: "Given two distinct points, there is one and only one line that passes through them." Our assumption breaks this rule!
Step 5: Conclusion
Since our assumption leads to an impossible contradiction, our assumption must be false. Therefore, two distinct lines cannot have more than one point in common. (Q.E.D.)

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: What does "Q.E.D." mean at the end of a mathematical proof?
It stands for Quod Erat Demonstrandum, which is Latin for "that which was to be demonstrated." Mathematicians write it at the very end of a proof to signal a mic drop—it means "I have successfully proven what I set out to prove!"
Q2: Why do we use Proof by Contradiction instead of just proving it directly?
Sometimes proving something directly is incredibly difficult because you have to account for infinite possibilities. However, proving that the opposite scenario is physically or logically impossible immediately forces the original statement to be true. It's a clever shortcut!
