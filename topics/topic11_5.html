🔄 Conversion of Solids

Melting, molding, and the conservation of volume!

When we melt a solid metallic shape and cast it into a completely new shape, its surface area will drastically change. However, the actual amount of metal does not disappear! This leads us to the most important rule of this topic:

The Golden Rule of Conversion

Volume of Original Solid = Volume of New Solid

(This also applies to pouring liquids from one container to another!)

🔥 1. The Melting Simulator

We are melting a metallic sphere to form a solid cylinder. Adjust the sphere's radius to change the total metal available, then choose how thick you want your new cylinder to be. Watch how the cylinder's height is forced to change to conserve the volume!

Original Volume 904.8
Resulting Cylinder Height (h) 18.0 h = Volume ÷ (πR₂²)

🏭 2. The Multi-Caster

What if we pour a large cylinder of juice into multiple small conical glasses? Adjust the dimensions to visually scale the objects and calculate exactly how many glasses you can fill!
Formula: Number of objects (N) = Volume of Large Solid ÷ Volume of One Small Solid.

Glasses that can be fully filled (N = Big Vol ÷ Small Vol) 54 (with leftover juice)

🕳️ 3. The Hollow Pipe Caster

Now let's do the reverse! We are melting a Large Solid Sphere to form a Hollow Cylindrical Pipe. You set the outer (R) and inner (r) thickness of the pipe, and the math will dictate how long (Height) the pipe will be!

Resulting Pipe Height (H = Sphere Vol ÷ π(R² - r²)) 10.67

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: A metallic sphere of radius 4.2 cm is melted and recast into the shape of a solid cylinder of radius 6 cm. Find the height of the cylinder.
Step 1: Volume of Sphere = (4/3) × π × (4.2)³
Step 2: Volume of Cylinder = π × (6)² × h
Step 3: Equate the volumes: π × 36 × h = (4/3) × π × (4.2)³
Step 4: Cancel π from both sides and solve for h.
h = [ (4/3) × 74.088 ] / 36
Final Answer: h = 2.744 cm
Q2: How many silver coins, 1.75 cm in diameter and of thickness 2 mm, must be melted to form a cuboid of dimensions 5.5 cm × 10 cm × 3.5 cm?
Step 1: A coin is a solid cylinder. Radius (r) = 1.75/2 = 0.875 cm. Height (h) = 2 mm = 0.2 cm.
Step 2: Volume of one coin = πr²h = π × (0.875)² × 0.2 ≈ 0.48125 cm³.
Step 3: Volume of cuboid = l × w × h = 5.5 × 10 × 3.5 = 192.5 cm³.
Step 4: Number of coins (N) = Volume of Cuboid ÷ Volume of one coin.
N = 192.5 ÷ 0.48125
Final Answer: 400 coins!
