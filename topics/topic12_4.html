Probability in the Real World

Theoretical probability doesn't just apply to single coin tosses. It allows us to calculate the likelihood of pulling specific items from a large mixed group, testing for defects, or finding the sum of multiple random events.

Scenario How to Calculate
Geometric Probability (Spinners) Probability = (Area of the Favorable Sector) / (Total Area of the Circle). Or simply: Sector Angle / 360°
Deck of Cards A standard deck has 52 cards (26 Red, 26 Black). Probability is based on counting specific subsets: 4 Suits (13 cards each), 4 Aces, 12 Face Cards (J, Q, K).
Compound Events (Two Dice) To find the probability of a specific sum (like rolling an 8), you must count every possible combination of the two dice that adds up to that number, divided by 36 total outcomes.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: What is the probability of randomly picking a Vowel from the word "MATHEMATICS"?
Step 1: Count the total number of letters. There are 11 letters total.
Step 2: Count the number of vowels (A, E, I, O, U). In "MATHEMATICS", the vowels are A, E, A, I. That's 4 vowels.
Step 3: Apply the formula P(E) = Favorable / Total.
Final Answer: 4 / 11.
Q2: A bag contains 3 red balls, 5 blue balls, and 2 green balls. What is the probability of NOT drawing a blue ball?
Step 1: Find the total number of balls: 3 + 5 + 2 = 10 balls.
Step 2: Count the favorable outcomes for "NOT blue". This means drawing Red or Green. 3 + 2 = 5 balls.
Step 3: P(Not Blue) = 5 / 10.
Step 4: Simplify the fraction.
Final Answer: 1 / 2 (or 50%).
