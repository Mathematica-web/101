📏 Lines Parallel to the Axes

When one variable goes missing, lines go straight!

Special Case Linear Equations

Not every linear equation features both x and y visibly. When an equation only contains one variable (like x = 3 or y = -2), it actually represents a straight line that perfectly mimics one of the coordinate axes.

Equation Form Geometric Behavior
x = a A perfectly vertical line parallel to the Y-axis. Every point on this line has an x-coordinate of exactly "a", regardless of its height.
y = b A perfectly horizontal line parallel to the X-axis. Every point on this line has a y-coordinate of exactly "b", regardless of how far left or right it goes.
x = 0 This is the exact equation of the Y-axis itself!
y = 0 This is the exact equation of the X-axis itself!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Does the line y = 7 ever cross the X-axis?
No, it never crosses!

The line y = 7 is perfectly horizontal and parallel to the X-axis. Because parallel lines never intersect, it will remain exactly 7 units above the X-axis for eternity.
Q2: Can a line be parallel to both the X-axis and the Y-axis at the same time?
Impossible!

The X and Y axes are perpendicular to each other (they meet at a perfect 90-degree angle). Therefore, any line that is parallel to the X-axis must automatically be perpendicular to the Y-axis, and vice versa.
