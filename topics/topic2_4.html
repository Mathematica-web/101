✂️ Factorisation of Polynomials

Breaking down complex equations into simple puzzle pieces!

What is Factorisation?

Factorisation is the exact reverse of multiplication. Instead of expanding a polynomial, we are breaking it apart into its simplest building blocks (factors) so that when you multiply them together, you get the original polynomial back.

Method How it Works
Splitting the Middle Term Used for quadratics like ax² + bx + c. You must find two magic numbers (p and q) that add up to 'b' but multiply to 'a × c'.
Factor Theorem If you can guess a zero of the polynomial (let's call it a) such that p(a) = 0, then (x - a) is guaranteed to be a factor!

🚀 3. Masterclass: Higher-Degree Factors

The Factor Theorem is great for checking simple linear factors like (x - 2). But what happens if you need to check if a massive quadratic like x² - 16x + 27 is a factor of an even bigger polynomial like x⁶ - 3x⁴ + 43x - 26?

Method 1: Polynomial Long Division (The Universal Method)

Because the divisor is a quadratic (degree 2), you cannot simply plug a single number into the polynomial. Instead, you must perform standard algebraic long division.

  • Step 1: Set up x⁶ - 3x⁴ + 43x - 26 inside the division bracket, and x² - 16x + 27 on the outside.
  • Step 2: Divide term by term, subtracting at each level just like regular long division.
  • The Verdict: If your final remainder at the very bottom is exactly 0, then the quadratic IS a perfect factor! If there is any x or constant left over, it is NOT a factor.

Method 2: The Double-Root Shortcut (If Possible)

If you are lucky, you might be able to factorize the quadratic divisor first!

  • Step 1: Try to split the divisor into two linear terms: (x - a)(x - b).
  • Step 2: Use the Factor Theorem twice! Check if P(a) = 0 AND P(b) = 0.
  • Note: In the case of x² - 16x + 27, the roots are irrational decimals, making Method 2 too messy. You must use Long Division!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Factorize the quadratic polynomial: x² - 7x + 12.
Step 1: Find two numbers that add up to the middle term (-7) and multiply to the constant (12).
Step 2: Test factors of 12: (-3) and (-4).
(-3) + (-4) = -7. (-3) × (-4) = 12. Perfect!
Step 3: Split the middle term: x² - 3x - 4x + 12.
Step 4: Factor by grouping: x(x - 3) - 4(x - 3).
Final Answer: (x - 3)(x - 4)
Q2: If (x - 2) is a factor of p(x) = x² - 5x + k, find the value of k.
Step 1: Use the Factor Theorem! If (x - 2) is a factor, then p(2) must exactly equal 0.
Step 2: Plug x = 2 into the polynomial.
(2)² - 5(2) + k = 0
4 - 10 + k = 0
-6 + k = 0
Step 3: Solve for k.
Final Answer: k = 6.
