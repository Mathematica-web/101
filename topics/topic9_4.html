🔄 Cyclic Quadrilaterals

When four vertices perfectly align on a single circle!

The Concyclic Rule

A quadrilateral is called cyclic if all four of its vertices lie exactly on the circumference of a circle. These vertices are said to be concyclic. Because the points are trapped on the circle, their angles are governed by strict supplementary laws.

Theorem The Rule
Theorem 9.10 The sum of either pair of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is exactly 180° (they are supplementary).
Theorem 9.11 (Converse) If the sum of a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is 180°, the quadrilateral is cyclic.

📐 1. The 180° Balance

Drag points C and D around the circle's circumference. Notice how the opposing pairs of angles independently shift in size, but their totals perfectly mathematically balance out to 180°!

Opposite Pair 1 ∠A + ∠C = 180°
Opposite Pair 2 ∠B + ∠D = 180°

💡 Why does this work? (The Proof)

Imagine drawing lines from the center of the circle to points B and D. This creates a central angle for arc BCD, and a reflex central angle for arc DAB. By the Double Angle Theorem, the angle at the circumference (∠A) is exactly half the central angle of arc BCD. Similarly, ∠C is exactly half the reflex central angle of arc DAB. Since the two central angles combine to form a full circle (360°), the sum of their halves (∠A + ∠C) must perfectly equal half of 360°, which is 180°!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, if ∠A is 110°, what is the measurement of ∠C?
70°.

Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary (they add up to 180°). Therefore, 180° - 110° = 70°.
Q2: Can a standard rhombus be a cyclic quadrilateral?
Only if it is a square.

A cyclic quadrilateral requires opposite angles to sum to 180°. In a standard rhombus, opposite angles are equal but not necessarily 90°, meaning they won't sum to 180° unless all four angles are exactly 90°.
