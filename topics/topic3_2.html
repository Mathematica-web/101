📏 The Distance Formula

Using Pythagoras to measure the distance between any two points!

Unlocking the Grid

How do we find the exact distance between two points on the Cartesian Plane? We draw a right-angled triangle between them and use the magic of Pythagoras' Theorem ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$).

Component Definition
Horizontal Distance (dx) The difference between the x-coordinates: x₂ - x₁
Vertical Distance (dy) The difference between the y-coordinates: y₂ - y₁
The Distance Formula (d) d = √[ (x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)² ]

📐 1. The Pythagorean Distance Tracker

Move Point A and Point B anywhere on the grid. Notice how they always form a hidden right-angled triangle ending at intersection C. The distance is just the hypotenuse!

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Find the distance between the origin (0,0) and the point (3,4).
Step 1: Identify the coordinates: (x₁, y₁) = (0,0) and (x₂, y₂) = (3,4).
Step 2: Calculate the differences.
dx = x₂ - x₁ = 3 - 0 = 3
dy = y₂ - y₁ = 4 - 0 = 4
Step 3: Plug them into the formula.
d = √(3² + 4²) = √(9 + 16) = √25
Final Answer: The distance is 5 units.
Q2: Can the distance between two points ever be a negative number?
No, never!

Because we square the horizontal and vertical differences (dx² and dy²), any negative numbers become positive. Since we are adding two positive numbers and taking the principal square root, distance is always a positive length (or 0 if the points are in the exact same spot).
