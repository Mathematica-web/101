The Number Family Tree

Every number you have ever learned belongs to a specific category. As you move down the list, the categories get larger and include all the groups above them!

Category Definition & Examples
Natural Numbers (N) Counting numbers starting from 1.
Examples: 1, 2, 3, 4...
Whole Numbers (W) All Natural Numbers, plus zero.
Examples: 0, 1, 2, 3...
Integers (Z) All Whole Numbers, plus their negative counterparts.
Examples: ...-2, -1, 0, 1, 2...
Rational Numbers (Q) Any number that can be written as a fraction p/q (where p and q are integers, and q ≠ 0).
Examples: 1/2, -3/4, 5 (since 5=5/1), 0.75
Irrational Numbers Numbers that CANNOT be written as a simple fraction. Their decimals go on forever without repeating.
Examples: π, √2, √3
Real Numbers (R) The ultimate collection! Every Rational and Irrational number combined.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Is zero (0) a rational number? Can you write it in the form p/q?
Yes, zero is a rational number!

A rational number must be writable as p/q, where p and q are integers and q is not zero.
We can write zero as 0/1, 0/5, or 0/-12.
Since p=0 (an integer) and q=1 (an integer not equal to zero), it perfectly fits the definition!
Q2: Without performing long division, determine if 13/3125 will have a terminating or non-terminating repeating decimal expansion.
Step 1: Look at the denominator (q = 3125).
Step 2: Find the prime factorization of 3125.
3125 = 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 = 5&sup5;.
Step 3: Apply the rule. Since the prime factors of the denominator consist only of the number 5, the fraction will have a Terminating decimal expansion.
