The Special Lines of a Triangle

Beyond the three basic sides, a triangle hides an entire network of special internal lines. These lines balance the shape, determine its height, and divide its angles with perfect mathematical precision.

Special Line Definition & Properties
Median A line connecting a vertex to the exact midpoint of the opposite side. The three medians of a triangle always meet at a single balancing point called the Centroid.
Altitude A line dropped from a vertex that hits the opposite side at a perfect 90° right angle. It represents the height of the triangle. The three altitudes meet at the Orthocenter.
Angle Bisector A line that slices an interior angle perfectly in half. The Angle Bisector Theorem states that it divides the opposite side into segments proportional to the other two sides.

✂️ 3. The Angle Bisector Theorem

When you slice an interior angle perfectly in half, the bisector hits the opposite side at a very specific point. It divides the base into a ratio that is perfectly identical to the ratio of the other two sides: AB / AC = BD / DC.

Side Ratio AB / AC = 1.00
Base Segment Ratio BD / DC = 1.00

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

Q1: Can an altitude ever fall outside the triangle?
Yes!

In an obtuse-angled triangle, if you drop an altitude from one of the acute angles to the opposite base, the base must be extended outward to meet the altitude at a 90° angle. The simulator demonstrates this perfectly!
Q2: Are the median and altitude ever the exact same line?
Yes, in specific cases!

In an isosceles triangle, the median drawn to the base is also the altitude and the angle bisector. In an equilateral triangle, all three medians are also the altitudes and angle bisectors!
